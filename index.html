Uso Car Club · Low Riders

Low and slow
since ’92

This is where it started: ten lowriders rolling out of Scott Park in Carson in August 1992. Candy paint, wire wheels, hydraulics and a plaque in the back window that means something. Two-time Lowrider Magazine Club of the Year, and still one of the biggest lowrider clubs in the world.

Shows, cruises & BBQsLowrider shows, cruises and BBQs with the family. When a chapter rolls out, it goes on Uso Announcements first. Pick Low Riders up there to see just the car side.
The plaqueUso is Samoan for blood brother, and it means what a brother means: someone who shows up, who has your back, who you can call at 2 a.m. That is the word in the back window. The plaque is not a logo, it is a promise to the people riding next to you. It is why getting in, in Kita’s words, “relied more on the person than the lowrider. Of course, the rides had to be clean as well.”
No color linesThe first ten members were already a mix of races. Kita said the club earned its respect “through our no color line concept.” That never changed.
Uso Car Club · Motorcycles · Since 2008

Two wheels.
Same blood.

The motorcycle branch of the Uso Car Club, established in 2008. Not a separate club and not an MC: the same family, riding under the same plaque. Chrome, paint, patience and pride, just on a different frame. When the family rolls out, the bikes protect the pack.

Ride togetherClub runs, memorial rides and escorts for the car club's biggest shows are posted on Uso Announcements first.
RepresentThe patch is earned, worn with respect and never disgraces the name Kita built.
Family firstUso means brother. That's the whole rulebook. Everything else follows from it.
Our Story

From Scott Park
to the whole world

Kita

KITA SILIVA LEALAO

1958 — 2020

Founder of the Uso Car Club. Born in Santa Clara, raised in the San Francisco Bay Area, a Cadillac man through and through: his first club was Low Creations of San Francisco, rolling a ’67 Cadillac. Thirty years on the ramp for TWA and American Airlines. He set out to erase the line between lowriding and the stigma that shadowed it, and built a family in the process.

No color lines.
1992Founded Uso
2×Club of the Year
HOFLowrider Hall of Fame

More than 600 people came to his celebration of life outside Sacramento. The family rides in his name every year.

1992

August, Scott Park, Carson

Over dinner at Kita’s house, he and Daniel Hunkin decide to start their own club. Ten members roll out of Scott Park in the Harbor Area of Los Angeles. "Uso" is Samoan for blood brother. The membership is mixed from day one, and the requirement for getting in is the person, not the car. The rides still had to be clean.

1990s

"No Color Lines"

The early motto pushes the club, and lowriding itself, past the boundaries of neighborhood and flag colors. Chapters start opening across California and then out of state.

1997

Club of the Year

Lowrider Magazine names Uso its Club of the Year, the first of two. The magazine runs its first full feature on the club the next April.

2002

Ten years

For the tenth anniversary the family adopts the motto "Same Team, Same Scheme, Same Dream." Same brothers, same blood.

2008

Hall of Fame, and two wheels

Kita is inducted into the Lowrider Hall of Fame with the Leadership Honor at the fourth annual ceremony in Long Beach. The same year the family adds its motorcycle branch. By then he had counted 42 chapters at one time and a second Club of the Year.

2016

Tribal King

After his Cadillac is totaled, the family buys and builds him a 1981 Coupe De Ville. It debuts at the Las Vegas Super Show for the club’s 25th anniversary.

2020

Rest easy, Kita

Kita passes on February 8, 2020 in Elk Grove, California, at 61. More than 600 people fill his celebration of life in Roseville, outside Sacramento, two weeks before the world shut down.

2022

Thirty years

The family comes home to Carson for the 30th anniversary. Detroit, flying the plaque since 2007, marks 15 years the same summer.

Today

Thirty-plus chapters, worldwide

Hawaii to Australia, Sweden to Guam, Alaska to Canada, and up and down California. And since 2008, the motorcycles riding alongside. This site is where the whole family looks to see what's next.
